Caption

Layout
Reply with a JSON object with exactly these fields: "width", "height", "bbox_coord_space", "bbox_format", "summary", and "items".
- "width" and "height": integers equal to the TARGET CANVAS in the request. Never change them.
- "bbox_coord_space": exactly "abs". "bbox_format": exactly "xyxy".
- "summary": an object with "caption" (a nonempty global image description, including the overall treatment), optionally "meta" (an array of medium or genre tags), "canvas" ("single", "multi_panel", or "collage"), and "palette" (an array of strings).
- "items": a nonempty flat array. Each item has "label" ("object", "character", or "text"), a nonempty "caption" describing its visible appearance (for text: typography and surface treatment), and "bbox". A text item also requires "text": the exact visible characters, with intended line breaks as \n. Items may also have "tags" and "palette" string arrays when they add useful visual information. Add no other fields: no ids, children, parents, elements, or notes.
- "bbox" is [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max] in actual pixels from the canvas top-left, with x within 0..width and y within 0..height and positive width and height. Do not use normalized coordinates, and do not center, reorder, or quantize boxes.

Use boxes for coherent visible units, splitting only when independent placement matters. Grouping should preserve content and relationships, not erase them. Natural occlusion and overlap are allowed, and several items may share a box. A box is a placement annotation, not an instruction to draw a border, panel, or sticker. Describe appearance in item captions and global treatment in summary.caption, and make the boxes agree with any spatial descriptions. Positions are global canvas coordinates, including text on an object.

Check every visible string against the requested wording. Check that line breaks, text length, and glyph scale fit each box at the declared resolution: a short Chinese title needs a different footprint from a paragraph of Latin text. Enlarge or recompose an unreadable layout rather than deleting copy or stretching glyphs. For a knowledge graphic, write actual explanations and explanatory imagery instead of empty modules. For a poster, keep its visual identity and information hierarchy rather than forcing an infographic structure.
